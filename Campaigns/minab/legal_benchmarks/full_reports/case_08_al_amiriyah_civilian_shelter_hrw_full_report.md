## 1. Executive Summary & Factual Record

At 04:30 AM local time on February 13, 1991, during week four of Operation Desert Storm, two USAF F-117A stealth aircraft belonging to the 37th Tactical Fighter Wing released two GBU-27 laser-guided bunker-buster bombs onto Public Air-Raid Shelter No. 25 in the middle-class residential district of Al-Amiriyah in Baghdad.

The structure was an underground reinforced-concrete civilian shelter designed and constructed between 1984 and 1985 by a Finnish/Swedish engineering consortium (*Perusyhtymä Oy* / *Vake* / *Lohja*) as part of a nationwide civil defense program initiated during the Iran-Iraq war. It was clearly designated as a public neighborhood air-raid shelter, equipped with beds, nursery areas, kitchenettes, and water storage.

### The Blast Dynamics & Lethal Sequence
1. **First Impact (Penetration & Confinement):** The initial 2,000-lb GBU-27 penetrated 3 meters (10 feet) of reinforced concrete through a roof ventilation duct and detonated in the upper floor concourse. The explosive overpressure collapsed interior blast baffles and blew shut the steel outer blast doors from within, automatically sealing occupants inside and locking the exit hatches.
2. **Second Impact (Interior Incineration):** Approximately four minutes later, the second GBU-27 followed the precise laser guidepath of the first bomb, entering directly through the breach created in the ceiling. It penetrated down into the lower dormitory floor where hundreds of women and children were sleeping. The warhead detonation shattered the municipal water boilers and auxiliary fuel tanks, superheating the enclosed environment and releasing superheated steam and fire. Over 400 individuals were incinerated or asphyxiated; only between 14 and 26 individuals in the outer airlocks survived.

## 2. The U.S. Military Defense vs. On-Site Investigative Truth

### The Pentagon Narrative
In immediate post-strike briefings, senior military officials—including Joint Staff Director of Operations Lt. Gen. Thomas Kelly, CENTCOM Deputy Director of Operations Brig. Gen. Richard Neal, and Secretary of Defense Dick Cheney—vigorously defended the strike:
* **The "Command Bunker" Allegation:** The Pentagon asserted that the shelter had been converted into an active Iraqi military command-and-control communications center.
* **Signals Intelligence (SIGINT):** Officials claimed intelligence satellites and interception units had detected active military electronic transmissions and telecommunications traffic originating from the structure.
* **Physical Camouflage & Perimeter Security:** Surveillance imagery was cited showing camouflage paint on the facility’s roof, concertina/barbed wire perimeter fencing, and armed guards.
* **Civilian Immunity Denial:** The military denied knowing civilians were sheltering in the facility, claiming either that Iraq had vacated all civilians or was deliberately utilizing civilians as "human shields" over a command facility.

### The Human Rights Watch (Middle East Watch) Field Investigation
Human Rights Watch conducted an exhaustive factual inquiry, synthesizing witness statements from neighborhood survivors, hospital triage registries, and immediate post-strike forensic examinations conducted by independent foreign correspondents (including Peter Arnett of CNN, Jeremy Bowen of the BBC, and Associated Press journalists):
1. **Total Absence of Military Communications Hardware:** Inspection of the debris revealed no military desks, communications consoles, cryptographic gear, antennas, or military uniforms. The physical wreckage consisted entirely of civilian items: baby bottles, cribs, strollers, cooking pots, blankets, and children’s toys.
2. **Established Neighborhood "Pattern of Life":** The facility was a well-known neighborhood civil defense shelter. For weeks prior to February 13, hundreds of neighborhood women and children queued and entered the facility every evening before curfew to sleep away from Coalition bombing runs, while fathers and older boys remained at home to protect property from looters. This predictable civilian movement was visible to basic optical or human surveillance.
3. **Pre-Existing Camouflage:** The camouflage on the roof was not an operational military deception applied during the Gulf War; it had been applied to all public municipal structures across Baghdad years earlier during the Iran-Iraq war.

## 3. Substantive Legal Determinations under International Humanitarian Law

In *Needless Deaths in the Gulf War*, Human Rights Watch formulated several definitive legal determinations that have become canonical benchmarks in IHL:

### A. The Irrebuttable Presumption of Civilian Status (AP I Art. 52(3))
Under Article 52(3) of Additional Protocol I, objects normally dedicated to civilian purposes (places of worship, houses, schools, civil defense shelters) are presumed not to be used for military purposes. Human Rights Watch held that a belligerent cannot extinguish this protected character based on speculative, ambiguous, or uncorroborated electronic signals. The burden of positive, real-time proof rests entirely upon the attacking force.

### B. Gross Breach of the Precautionary Mandate (AP I Art. 57)
Under Article 57(2)(a)(i), attackers must do everything feasible to verify that objectives are military objectives. Planners breached this affirmative duty by relying exclusively on remote SIGINT and detached photo-interpretation without conducting basic pattern-of-life surveillance during twilight hours when hundreds of civilians openly entered the shelter.

### C. Violation of the Mandatory Advance Warning Requirement (AP I Art. 57(2)(c) & Art. 65(2))
Civil defense shelters are accorded special protection under international law. Under Article 65 of Additional Protocol I, even if a civil defense facility is suspected of being used to commit acts harmful to the enemy, its immunity ceases *only after an advance warning with a reasonable time-limit has been given and remained unheeded*. The total failure of the United States to give an advance warning before attacking Public Shelter No. 25 constituted an unexcused, serious violation of customary IHL.

### D. Weapons Disproportionality in Enclosed Civilian Spaces (AP I Art. 51(5)(b))
Even if an auxiliary military communications unit were operating within the structure, deploying two 2,000-lb bunker busters into a reinforced concrete civilian enclosure packed with 400+ sleeping women and children was grossly disproportionate. Delivering penetrating heavy ordnance into a trapped civilian environment makes mass death mathematically certain, far outweighing any conceivable tactical military advantage.

## 4. Upstream Allocation of Responsibility & The "Checkmate" Target Cell

The targeting of Shelter 25 was not an error of pilot navigation or tactical aircrew release. The F-117A stealth pilots merely flew assigned mission routes and designated the laser energy onto coordinates supplied by the target nomination sheet.

Responsibility lay entirely upstream within the target planning architecture:
* The **Checkmate** air campaign planning cell in the Pentagon and Riyadh;
* Intelligence officers who interpreted uncorroborated SIGINT as proof of military conversion without demanding visual ground verification;
* The Joint Force Air Component Commander (JFACC) and senior CENTCOM leadership who approved the target package without verifying civilian occupancy.

Following the international outrage caused by the Amiriyah massacre, General Colin Powell and General Norman Schwarzkopf severely restricted airstrikes in central Baghdad, requiring specific approval by the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff for any target within the capital.
