# Case 15: Dahyan Market School Bus Airstrike (Sa'ada, Yemen, August 9, 2018)
### Comprehensive Judicial Benchmark & Evidentiary Dossier
> **Authoritative Fact-Finding Bodies:** United Nations Group of Eminent International and Regional Experts on Yemen (UN Doc. A/HRC/42/17 & A/HRC/42/CRP.1); Joint Incident Assessment Team (JIAT Press Release, 1 September 2018); International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC); Human Rights Watch (HRW); Amnesty International; Mwatana for Human Rights & Columbia Law School Human Rights Institute; Bellingcat Investigation Team.  
> **Date & Time of Attack:** Thursday, 9 August 2018, approximately 08:30–08:45 local AST.  
> **Geographic Location:** Central Commercial Marketplace of Dahyan, Majz District, Sa'ada Governorate, Northern Yemen (`17.0694° N, 43.6192° E`).  
> **Target:** A 30-seat blue civilian passenger bus carrying approximately 54 young schoolboys returning from a Quranic summer camp excursion, stopped in front of a market butcher shop to buy drinks.  
> **Human Casualties:** 51 certified dead (including 40 schoolchildren aged 6 to 13, and 11 adult market bystanders and teachers); 79 severely injured (including 56 children, suffering extensive blast amputations, skull fractures, and third-degree thermal burns).  
> **Weapon Deployed:** GBU-12 Paveway II 500-lb (227 kg) laser-guided bomb (Mk 82 bomb body fitted with MXU-650 airfoil and MAU-169 computer control group), manufactured by Lockheed Martin (Orlando, Florida, CAGE Code `94117`) and procured under US Foreign Military Sales (FMS).  
> **Applicable Evidentiary & Legal Benchmarks:** Principle of Distinction (AP I Arts. 48, 51, 52); Affirmative Duty of Precautions in Attack and Non-Delegable Duty to Abort (AP I Art. 57(2)(a) & (b); CIHL Rules 15–21); Weapons Proportionality (AP I Art. 51(5)(b); Rome Statute Art. 8(2)(b)(iv); CIHL Rule 14); Mechanical Perfection of Precision Munitions; Heightened Sanctuary Protection for Children (Geneva Convention IV Art. 24; AP I Art. 77; CRC Art. 38; UNSC Res 1612 & 2601; Safe Schools Declaration); Belligerent Concession of ROE Breach (JIAT Official Concession); Weapons Exporter Complicity & State Responsibility (ATT Art. 7; ILC ARSIWA Arts. 16, 31, 35, 36).

## 1. Factual Background & Chronological Sequence of the Incident

1. On the morning of Thursday, 9 August 2018, approximately 54 boys aged 6 to 13 from the local Quranic summer school program in Sa'ada participated in an educational day-trip. The itinerary included visiting a local cemetery and a nearby valley picnic area.
2. The children traveled in a clearly identifiable, non-armored blue civil passenger bus. Prior to the strike, the children were filmed laughing, reciting Quranic verses, and joking inside the bus.
3. At approximately 08:30 local time, the bus paused along the main commercial thoroughfare of the Dahyan market in Majz District, Sa'ada Governorate. The stop was intended to allow the children to disembark or purchase snacks and bottled water from roadside grocery and butcher stalls.
4. The market was densely occupied by civilians conducting morning shopping. There were no anti-aircraft emplacements, military barricades, active skirmishes, or visible military equipment within the immediate market perimeter.
5. While stationary in front of a butcher shop, a single high-precision laser-guided aerial bomb dropped by a Saudi/UAE-led Coalition combat aircraft detonated directly upon the roof and front section of the bus.
6. The catastrophic detonation resulted in the instantaneous vaporization and dismemberment of the children seated in the forward compartments, tore the vehicle into shredded scrap metal, and projected supersonic fragmentation across the surrounding market stalls, killing 51 people (including 40 children) and maiming 79 others.

## 2. Technical Weapons Forensics & Open-Source Physical Tracing

1. **Munition Identification:** Physical wreckage, fin assembly components, and electronic circuit housings were recovered from the crater and photographed by local journalists, international broadcast networks (CNN), and human rights investigators (Mwatana, Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International).
2. **CAGE Code & Contract Markings:** Detailed open-source analysis conducted by the **Bellingcat Investigation Team** and verified by international ordnance specialists identified the Commercial and Government Entity (CAGE) code stamped upon the control section:
   - `CAGE 94117`: Lockheed Martin Corporation (Missiles and Fire Control, Orlando, Florida);
   - Munition Type: **GBU-12 Paveway II** 500-lb Laser-Guided Bomb (utilizing an Mk 82 general-purpose warhead containing 87 kg of Tritonal high explosive);
   - Guidance Telemetry: Semi-active laser guidance system with sub-meter Circular Error Probable (CEP), designed to track the reflected laser energy emitted by an airborne targeting pod.
3. **The Mechanical Perfection Finding:** The GBU-12 guided directly to its designated aimpoint. There was no mechanical guidance failure, fin jam, or ballistic deflection. The weapon struck precisely where the aircrew's laser designator was aimed. Consequently, the catastrophic devastation was 100% attributable to the human targeting chain, intelligence authorization, and failure to exercise feasible precautions.

## 4. Substantive Legal Principles & Precedential Framework

The findings across these ten authoritative sources establish decisive international legal doctrines governing aerial targeting:

### A. The Principle of Distinction & Absolute Presumption of Civilian Status
- **Governing Law:** AP I Arts. 48, 51(2), 52(3); Customary IHL Rules 1, 7, 10; Rome Statute Art. 8(2)(b)(i) & (ii).
- **Judicial Standard:** An attacking force cannot treat an entire vehicle or location as a military objective based on uncorroborated intelligence or proximity to an adversary area. Under AP I Article 52(3), in case of doubt whether an object normally dedicated to civilian purposes (such as a passenger bus or school building) is being used to make an effective contribution to military action, it **must be presumed to be civilian**.
- **Case Law:** ICTY Trial Chamber in *Prosecutor v. Galić* (Judgment, IT-98-29-T, 5 December 2003, paras 48–55) affirmed that direct attacks on civilians or indiscriminate attacks launched with recklessness as to civilian casualties constitute war crimes.

### B. Affirmative Duty to Take Feasible Precautions & The Mandatory Abort Duty
- **Governing Law:** AP I Art. 57(2)(a)(i), (ii), and (b); Customary IHL Rules 15–21; DoD Law of War Manual § 5.11.
- **Judicial Standard:** Planners and commanders must do everything feasible to verify that the objectives to be attacked are military objectives. Crucially, under **AP I Article 57(2)(b)**, an attack **must be cancelled or suspended** if it becomes apparent that the objective is not a military one or that the attack may be expected to cause incidental loss of civilian life which would be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage expected.
- **The Dahyan Precedent:** The JIAT admission establishes that even where a target was originally nominated, a change in circumstances (an operational delay causing the bus to enter an active marketplace with children) triggers an **absolute, non-delegable duty to suspend or abort**. Failing to abort when the vehicle entered the market constitutes a gross breach of AP I Art. 57(2)(b) and customary IHL.

### C. Weapons Proportionality & Blast Radius in Populated Sanctuaries
- **Governing Law:** AP I Art. 51(5)(b); Rome Statute Art. 8(2)(b)(iv); Customary IHL Rule 14.
- **Judicial Standard:** An attack is disproportionate *per se* if the incidental loss of civilian life is excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated.
- **Case Law:** ECtHR Grand Chamber in *Isayeva v. Russia* (2005, paras 189–191) and *Isayeva, Yusupova and Bazayeva v. Russia* (2005, paras 195–199); ICTY Trial Chamber in *Prosecutor v. Kupreškić et al.* (Judgment, IT-95-16-T, 14 January 2000, paras 524–526). Deploying heavy blast-fragmentation munitions in the immediate presence of civilians and children cannot be justified by any speculative tactical advantage.

### D. Special Protections for Children & Educational Sanctuaries
- **Governing Law:** Geneva Convention IV Art. 24; AP I Art. 77; UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) Art. 38; UNSC Resolutions 1612 (2005), 1882 (2009), 1998 (2011), and 2601 (2021); Safe Schools Declaration (2015).
- **Judicial Standard:** Children enjoy heightened, peremptory protection in armed conflict. Attacking an educational transport or facility dedicated to children represents a grave breach of international humanitarian law.

### E. Secondary State Responsibility & Complicity in Arms Transfers
- **Governing Law:** Common Article 1 to the Geneva Conventions; Arms Trade Treaty (ATT) Arts. 6 & 7; ILC Articles on State Responsibility (ARSIWA) Art. 16.
- **Judicial Standard:** A State that aids or assists another State in the commission of an internationally wrongful act is internationally responsible if it has knowledge of the circumstances and the act would be internationally wrongful if committed by that State (ARSIWA Art. 16). Supplying precision munitions (Lockheed Martin GBU-12) to a belligerent with a documented pattern of unlawful airstrikes engages third-state responsibility.

### F. Mandatory Restitution & State Reparations
- **Governing Law:** Permanent Court of International Justice (PCIJ), *Factory at Chorzów* (1928, Ser. A, No. 17, p. 47); ILC ARSIWA Arts. 31, 35, 36.
- **Judicial Standard:** Any breach of an international obligation entails an autonomous duty to make full reparation (*restitutio in integrum*). Discretionary, unilateral *ex gratia* payments that deny legal liability do not satisfy the requirements of customary international law.
