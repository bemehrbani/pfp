# Case Dossier 06: Dora Farms Day 1 Strike (HRW / Iraq)
> **Database ID:** PFPJ-LB-006  
> **Applicable Aspects:** A (Precision Weaponry), B (Targeting Error), C (Protected Infrastructure), F (Day 1 / Pre-Planned Peacetime Nominations)

---

## 1. Incident Overview
*   **Date:** March 19–20, 2003
*   **Location:** Dora Farms, Baghdad, Iraq
*   **Weapon Used:** Satellite-guided JDAM bombs and Tomahawk cruise missiles.
*   **Casualties:** 1 civilian killed, 14 injured (including 9 women and a child).

---

## 2. Factual Breakdown & Targeting Error
Hours before the ground invasion of Iraq officially commenced, the US military launched a "decapitation strike" on a residential palace at Dora Farms. The attack was based on urgent CIA intelligence that Saddam Hussein and his sons were staying at the compound. 

The intelligence was completely incorrect; Saddam Hussein was not at the location, and the compound was occupied by civilian staff and their families.

---

## 3. Legal Proceedings & Public Reports
*   **Investigating Body:** Human Rights Watch.
*   **Public Report:** *Off Target: The Conduct of the War and Civilian Casualties in Iraq (December 2003)*.
*   **Public Report URL:** [Human Rights Watch Iraq Campaign Archive - Off Target](https://www.hrw.org/report/2003/12/11/target/conduct-war-and-civilian-casualties-iraq)

---

## 4. Key Legal Precedents & Rationale
*   **The Pre-Planned Day 1 Standard:** Because this was the opening salvo of the war, the targets were drawn from pre-arranged list profiles. The report established that for **Day 1 decapitation strikes**, planners cannot hide behind the "fog of war" or rapidly changing battlefield conditions.
*   **Evidentiary Failure in Intelligence:** Using heavy weapons on a target based solely on uncorroborated, single-source human intelligence (HUMINT) that is not cross-checked against satellite imagery of civilian occupancy violates target verification rules.

---

## 5. Direct Application to the Minab Case
*   **Day 1 Targeting Negligence:** The Minab strike occurred in the initial hours of the US-Iran war. Like the Dora Farms strike, the US military cannot claim the targeting was a product of battlefield confusion or reactive self-defense. The target (Minab base/school area) was a pre-planned coordinate. If the US used outdated coordinates on Day 1, it demonstrates a **pre-meditated failure of target verification during the peacetime planning phase**, which holds commanders directly liable for negligence under IHL.
