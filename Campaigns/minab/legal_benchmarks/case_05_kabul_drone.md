# Case Dossier 05: Kabul Drone Strike (US CENTCOM)
> **Database ID:** PFPJ-LB-005  
> **Applicable Aspects:** A (Precision Weaponry), B (Targeting Error), C (Protected Infrastructure), D (Proportionality & Proximity)

---

## 1. Incident Overview
*   **Date:** August 29, 2021
*   **Location:** Kabul, Afghanistan
*   **Weapon Used:** AGM-114 Hellfire missile (deployed from an MQ-9 Reaper drone).
*   **Casualties:** 10 civilians killed, including 7 children.

---

## 2. Factual Breakdown & Targeting Error
In the final days of the US withdrawal from Afghanistan, US drone operators tracked a white Toyota Corolla, believing it was loaded with explosives for an ISIS-K attack on Kabul airport. The vehicle belonged to Zemari Ahmadi, an employee of a US-based humanitarian aid group. 

Drone operators interpreted his daily activities—such as picking up co-workers and loading heavy water containers into his car—as hostile preparation. They authorized a precision missile strike as the car parked in a residential courtyard.

## 4. Key Legal Precedents & Rationale
*   **Confirmation Bias in Remote Intelligence:** The military investigation admitted the strike was a "tragic mistake" caused by **confirmation bias**—the tendency of intelligence analysts to interpret neutral civilian actions (such as loading water containers) as confirming their pre-existing threat hypothesis.
*   **Failure of Precaution in Final Launch Loop:** The strike was authorized despite civilian presence (children running out to greet Ahmadi's car) in the drone's active visual feed, demonstrating a failure of real-time target verification before weapon release.

---

## 5. Direct Application to the Minab Case
*   **Systemic Confirmation Bias:** The Kabul case provides the psychological and operational template for our report's critique of the US intelligence chain. It illustrates that when commanders operate under a high-threat state (Day 1 of the US-Iran war), they suffer from severe confirmation bias, interpreting the normal movement of children and staff at the school as IRGC naval base operations. This benchmark proves that remote surveillance without local verification is systemically flawed.
