# Case Dossier 03: Katyr-Yurt Convoy Strike (ECtHR / Russia)
> **Database ID:** PFPJ-LB-003  
> **Applicable Aspects:** A (Precision Weaponry), B (Targeting Error), D (Proportionality & Proximity), E (Universal Jurisdiction / Procedural Duty)

---

## 1. Incident Overview
*   **Date:** February 4–7, 2000
*   **Location:** Katyr-Yurt, Chechnya, Russian Federation
*   **Weapon Used:** Heavy S-24 aviation rockets (unguided but deployed with precision target aiming from Su-25 jets) and heavy FAB-250/FAB-500 bombs.
*   **Casualties:** 150+ civilians killed and injured.

---

## 2. Factual Breakdown & Targeting Error
Russian military forces conducted heavy aerial bombardments on the village of Katyr-Yurt and a civilian convoy fleeing the area under a designated safe passage corridor. The Russian military claimed they were targeting rebel fighters who had entered the village. They argued that the civilian casualties were a targeting error / collateral damage stemming from the presence of combatants inside the civilian zone.

---

## 3. Legal Proceedings & Public Reports
*   **Investigating Body:** European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR).
*   **Key Judgment:** *Isayeva v. Russia (Application no. 57950/00) - ECtHR Judgment (24 February 2005)*.
*   **Public Report URL:** [ECtHR HUDOC Portal - Isayeva v. Russia Judgment](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=001-68379)

---

## 4. Key Legal Precedents & Rationale
*   **The Principle of Precaution in Planning:** The ECtHR ruled that Russia violated Article 2 (Right to Life) of the ECHR. The Court held that even when targeting legitimate military threats (rebel fighters), the military operation was not **planned and executed with the necessary care** to protect civilian lives.
*   **Disproportionality of Weapons Choice:** The Court emphasized that deploying heavy high-explosive aviation weapons in a densely populated civilian zone, without verifying the presence of non-combatants, was disproportionate and did not constitute "absolutely necessary" force.

---

## 5. Direct Application to the Minab Case
*   **Pillar of Proportionality:** The US defense that the Minab school was struck because it was adjacent to the IRGC base mirrors Russia's defense in *Isayeva*. Our report can cite this case to argue that **weaponry selection and strike planning** must respect the proximity of civilians. Launching heavy precision missiles at a target sharing a fence line with an active girls' school is inherently disproportionate, regardless of the military value of the adjacent base.
