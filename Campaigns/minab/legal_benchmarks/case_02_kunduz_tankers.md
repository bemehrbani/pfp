# Case Dossier 02: Kunduz Tankers Airstrike (ECtHR / Germany)
> **Database ID:** PFPJ-LB-002  
> **Applicable Aspects:** A (Precision Weaponry), B (Targeting Error), D (Proportionality & Proximity), E (Universal Jurisdiction / Procedural Duty)

---

## 1. Incident Overview
*   **Date:** September 4, 2009
*   **Location:** Kunduz Province, Afghanistan
*   **Weapon Used:** Two GBU-38 JDAM precision-guided bombs (dropped by US F-15 jets).
*   **Casualties:** 90+ civilians killed, including children.

---

## 2. Factual Breakdown & Targeting Error
A German military commander, Colonel Klein, ordered US fighter aircraft to strike two fuel tankers that had been hijacked by Taliban insurgents. The tankers were stuck on a sandbank in the Kunduz River, and hundreds of local civilians had gathered to drain fuel from them. 

The German commander relied on a single intelligence source (a human informant) and poor thermal video feeds, mistakenly concluding that only insurgents were present. He authorized the strike, claiming a targeting error due to insufficient local intelligence and visual confirmation bias.

---

## 3. Legal Proceedings & Public Reports
*   **Investigating Bodies:** German Federal Prosecutor (Generalbundesanwalt), German Civil Courts (BGH), European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) Grand Chamber.
*   **Key Judgment:** *Hanan v. Germany (Application no. 4871/16) - ECtHR Grand Chamber Judgment (16 February 2021)*.
*   **Public Report URL:** [ECtHR HUDOC Portal - Hanan v. Germany Judgment](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=001-207908)

---

## 4. Key Legal Precedents & Rationale
*   **Universal Jurisdiction Investigation Obligation:** The ECtHR ruled that Germany had a binding **procedural duty under Article 2 ECHR (Right to Life)** to conduct an effective criminal investigation into the strike, even though it occurred in Afghanistan. This was because Germany retained exclusive disciplinary and criminal jurisdiction over its forces.
*   **The VGB (German Code of Crimes against International Law) Precedent:** The German Federal Prosecutor opened a formal war crimes investigation under domestic universal jurisdiction. While the case was eventually dismissed because they found no evidence that the commander acted with *intent* to kill civilians (the "good faith mistake" defense), the legal machinery of Universal Jurisdiction was fully established.

---

## 5. Direct Application to the Minab Case
*   **Universal Jurisdiction Avenue:** Because PFPJ ry is registered in Finland, we can use the ECHR procedural duty established in *Hanan* to petition Finnish or other European prosecutors to open a **Universal Jurisdiction war crimes investigation** into the US commanders who authorized the Minab strike. The precedent holds that the state whose military commands or coordinates the strike has a procedural obligation under European human rights law to investigate high-casualty civilian events.
