## 1. Incident Overview
*   **Date:** October 29, 1999 (~14:00–16:00 local time)
*   **Location:** Rostov–Baku ("Kavkaz") Highway near Shaami-Yurt, Chechnya, Russian Federation
*   **Weapon Used:** Twelve S-24 heavy unguided air-to-ground missiles (six fired per Su-25 attack jet; each missile weighing 235 kg, containing 123 kg HE warhead, generating over 4,000 fragments across a lethal dispersal radius of 300–800 metres).
*   **Casualties:** Dozens of certified civilian deaths (including children and two Chechen Red Cross workers), over 70 wounded, and numerous civilian vehicles incinerated.

---

## 2. Factual Breakdown & Targeting Failure
*   **The "Humanitarian Corridor" Trap:** Civilians were publicly instructed via Russian state television and radio that a humanitarian corridor would be open on October 29, 1999, to allow Grozny residents to flee to Ingushetia.
*   **The Checkpoint Reversal:** When thousands of refugees in a 12-kilometre-long column reached the "Kavkaz-1" checkpoint, federal soldiers closed the crossing and ordered the massive column of cars, buses, and Red Cross vehicles to turn back toward Grozny.
*   **The Air Strike:** Two Russian Su-25 attack aircraft conducted multiple passes over several hours, repeatedly launching S-24 heavy missiles into the traffic near Shaami-Yurt.
*   **The State Defense:** Russia claimed the pilots were attacked by insurgent firearms from a Kamaz truck, received clearance from ground air traffic control to strike in self-defense under ECHR Article 2(2)(a), and that civilian vehicles "suddenly entered" the blast zone or were being used as human shields.

## 4. Key Legal Precedents & Rationale
1.  **The Planning and Control Standard under Article 2 (§§ 171, 175, 177, 199):**
    State liability is engaged not merely by the tactical actions of pilots, but fundamentally by **whether the operation was planned and controlled by the authorities so as to minimise, to the greatest extent possible, recourse to lethal force and risk to civilian life**.
2.  **Institutional Information Siloing (§§ 185–187, 222):**
    The Court held that the total failure of communication between the authorities who announced the safe corridor, the roadblock officers who turned civilians around, and the air planners who scheduled combat sorties in ignorance of civilian presence constituted gross negligence in operational planning.
3.  **Weapons Disproportionality in Civilian Settings (§§ 195, 199):**
    Using twelve heavy blast-fragmentation missiles (blast radius 300–800 m) on a public road where thousands of civilians were present was manifestly disproportionate, rendering civilian slaughter mathematically inevitable.
4.  **Rebuttal of "Dynamic Target" / "Sudden Appearance" Defenses (§§ 192–194):**
    The Court found "insurmountable" the contradiction between pilot claims of targeting isolated trucks and the massive civilian reality. Given good weather, multiple low passes, and multi-hour engagement duration, the presence of civilians could not have been overlooked.
5.  **Autonomous Procedural Obligation to Investigate (§§ 208–225):**
    The Court established that delayed inquiries, unexamined flight logs/orders, failure to question senior checkpoint officers, and closed-door exoneration of military personnel without victim standing violate the non-derogable procedural limb of Article 2.

## 5. Direct Application to the Minab Factual Determination Report
*   **Chapter V & VII (§ 7.3 - Proportionality):** The deployment of three 1,000-lb WDU-36/B warheads (blast/fragmentation envelope > 300 m) 42 metres from an active elementary school mirrors the S-24 missile deployment in *Isayeva, Yusupova & Bazayeva*. Detonating heavy blast ordnance immediately adjacent to protected civilians makes mass child casualties mathematically certain and legally disproportionate *per se*.
*   **Chapter VI (§ 6.2 & § 6.5 - Targeting Silos & AI Velocity):** The disconnect between military database nominators and the civilian status of the school reproduces the institutional siloing condemned in §§ 185–187.
*   **Chapter VIII (§ 8.1 - Upstream Concentration of Liability):** Just as the ECtHR placed responsibility on the operational command and planners rather than the pilots who followed flight orders, liability for the Minab strike concentrates upstream on CENTCOM targeting boards and DIA intelligence nominators, completely exonerating tactical naval launch crews.
*   **Chapter IX (§ 9.3 - Procedural Duty to Investigate):** Establishes that internal military investigations that conceal logs and exclude victims breach the procedural standards of Article 2, grounding European universal jurisdiction filings.
