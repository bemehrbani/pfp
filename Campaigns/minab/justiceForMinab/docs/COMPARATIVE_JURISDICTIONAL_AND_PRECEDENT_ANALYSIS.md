## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & THE FOUR STRATEGIC QUESTIONS

Following the completion of PFPJ ry's **24 October 2026 Working Draft (`Draft v0.1`)** of the *Master Factual Determination Report* (incorporating 19 international legal benchmarks across `EXH-001` through `EXH-035`), **Maria Mekri** (Executive Director, SaferGlobe, `29–30 October 2026`) raised four foundational strategic questions regarding how a civil-society organization can realistically advance accountability when traditional forums face structural bottlenecks:

1. **Evidentiary Sufficiency:** *Is there enough material in the current dossier to take the case forward?*
2. **Forum Selection & Jurisdictional Creativity:** *Given that (a) an Iranian domestic judgment will be dismissed internationally as politically partisan, (b) Finland's judicial budget austerity makes a multi-million-euro extraterritorial trial unlikely in Finnish courts alone, and (c) the International Criminal Court (ICC) is overstretched and faces U.S./Iranian non-party hurdles, where have cases like this actually succeeded?*
3. **Primary Remedy vs. Systemic Legal Precedent:** *Should the legal strategy focus on financial compensation for the bereaved families of the 156 Minab school victims, or on establishing binding legal precedent that deploying military AI targeting pipelines without civilian guardrails constitutes an international humanitarian crime?*
4. **Litigation Cost & Institutional Champions:** *Given that full international litigation costs multiple millions of euros, which public prosecution units, strategic litigation NGOs, and policy coalitions have the mandate, procedural mechanisms, and resources to carry the case?*

### Summary of Core Conclusions

* **Conclusion 1 (The Public-Prosecutor Cost Shift):** In specialized European civil-law jurisdictions—specifically **France** (*Pôle crimes contre l'humanité*, Paris Judicial Court), **The Netherlands** (*Team Internationale Misdrijven* [TIM] & The Hague District Court), **Germany** (*Generalbundesanwalt* under *VStGB* § 1), and **Sweden** (*Krigsbrottskommissionen*)—civil-society NGOs and victims **do not fund a multi-million-euro trial**. Once an NGO/victim complaint (*plainte avec constitution de partie civile* or prosecutorial referral) establishes a credible *prima facie* nexus, **independent investigating magistrates (*juges d'instruction*) and public war-crimes prosecutors fund the forensic investigation, witness travel, expert commissions, and trial out of the State justice budget**.
* **Conclusion 2 (The Corporate Hardware & AI Software Territorial Hook):** Unlike pure "universal jurisdiction" against foreign military commanders (which faces sovereign and functional immunity barriers and political hesitation), **territorial and active-personality jurisdiction over corporate supply-chain and technology complicity** attaches directly to European-domiciled subsidiaries and parent companies whose hardware (`Raytheon/RTX`, `BAE Systems / Ball Aerospace`, `Allient / Globe Motors`) and AI software (`Palantir Technologies`, `Anthropic`) were physically recovered from or documented in the Minab strike.
* **Conclusion 3 (Dual-Track Remedy Architecture):** **Systemic AI precedent** and **victim compensation** are mutually reinforcing when split across two tracks:
  1. **The Systemic AI & Supply-Chain Precedent Track (European Courts & OECD NCPs):** Targeting the deployment of automated target-generation pipelines (*Palantir Maven Smart System + Claude AI*) stripped of human civilian-harm safeguards (90% reduction of CENTCOM's CHMR unit from 10 analysts to 1), followed by Palantir's post-strike emergency software patch. Under the French Supreme Court's landmark ***Lafarge*** standard (2021/2024) and the ***Amesys / Nexa*** and ***Exxelia / Eurofarad*** war-crimes inquiries, supplying dual-use software or precision guidance components with knowledge of foreseeable civilian slaughter constitutes actionable **complicity in war crimes**.
  2. **The Victim Reparation & Restitution Track:** Pursuing civil party damages (*dommages-intérêts* in French *partie civile* proceedings, Dutch Article 6:162 civil tort claims, and Swedish-style corporate forfeiture as in *Lundin Energy*), paired with U.S. Congressional oversight (**NDAA Section 1213** civilian harm funds) and a bilateral humanitarian trust fund modeled on the **$32.5 million *Belgrade Embassy* settlement (Case 01)**.

## 3. COMPARATIVE BENCHMARKING: 12 LANDMARK CASES WHERE SIMILAR ACTIONS SUCCEEDED

To answer Maria Mekri's core question—*"Find out where cases like this have successfully been taken forward and then see if that is a path you can take"*—this section benchmarks **twelve concrete cases across six jurisdictions** where victims and NGOs overcame political resistance and litigation cost barriers.

### A. FRANCE: The *Partie Civile* Investigative Magistrate Model (*Pôle crimes contre l'humanité*, Paris Judicial Court)

#### Why the French Mechanism Solves the Multi-Million-Euro Cost Problem
Under **Articles 85 and 88 of the French Code of Criminal Procedure (*Code de procédure pénale*)**, any direct victim (or an accredited human-rights association satisfying statutory standing requirements under Art. 2-4) may file a **criminal complaint with civil-party constitution (*plainte avec constitution de partie civile*)** directly before the specialized **War Crimes and Crimes Against Humanity Unit (*Pôle crimes contre l'humanité, crimes et délits de guerre*)** of the Judicial Court of Paris (*Tribunal judiciaire de Paris*).
- Unlike common-law private lawsuits where plaintiffs must fund tens of millions in discovery and expert fees, a *partie civile* filing (requiring only a modest refundable security deposit, *consignation*, typically €1,500–€5,000, or waived under legal aid) **legally compels the appointment of an independent investigating magistrate (*juge d'instruction*)**.
- Once seized, the **French State funds the investigation**, deploying the specialized investigators of the *Office central de lutte contre les crimes contre l'humanité et les crimes de haine (OCLCH)* to raid corporate offices in France, subpoena internal engineering emails and source code, issue international rogatory letters (*commissions rogatoires internationales*), and bring foreign victims to Paris to testify at public expense.

#### Case Benchmark F-1: *The Exxelia Technologies (Eurofarad) Missile Sensor Case* (Paris War Crimes Unit, 2016–Present)
* **Factual Parallel to Minab:** On 17 July 2014, during Operation Protective Edge, an aerial missile struck the roof of the Shuheibar family house in Gaza City while children were feeding pigeons, killing **three children** (Afnan, 8; Wassim, 9; Jihad, 10) and maiming two others. Among the recovered debris inside the courtyard, weapons investigators found a **Hall-effect position sensor** (used in missile guidance fins) stamped with the markings of **Eurofarad**, a French aerospace component manufacturer subsequently renamed **Exxelia Technologies** (headquartered in Paris).
* **Legal Mechanism & NGO Champions:** In June 2016, the Shuheibar family—represented by Paris law firm **Ancile Avocats** (Me Joseph Breham) and supported by **ACAT-France** and the **Al Mezan Center for Human Rights**—filed a criminal complaint before the Paris War Crimes Unit accusing Exxelia Technologies of **complicity in war crimes** (*complicité de crimes de guerre*, French Penal Code Arts. 121-7 & 461-1 *et seq.*) and **involuntary manslaughter** (*homicide involontaire*, Art. 221-6).
* **Outcome & Procedural Milestone:** In January 2018, French prosecutors opened a formal judicial investigation (*information judiciaire*) assigned to specialized investigating judges. In June 2019, the Paris investigating judge brought the surviving Shuheibar family members from Gaza to the Paris Judicial Court for formal in-person hearings—marking the first time victims of a Middle East airstrike testified before a European magistrate investigating a defense component manufacturer.
* **Direct Transposition to Minab:** In Minab, investigators recovered serialized guidance circuit boards (`CAGE 05047`), satellite data-link antennas (`CAGE 17810`), and fin actuator motors (`CAGE 55741`) from the school rubble where **120 elementary schoolgirls** were killed. Just as Eurofarad's sensor provided the jurisdictional anchor in Paris, the French corporate footprint of **Palantir Technologies France SAS** (Paris) and **RTX / Collins Aerospace France** (Toulouse/Paris) provides a direct French nexus.

#### Case Benchmark F-2: *Lafarge SA* — Corporate Indictment for Complicity in International Crimes (*Cour de cassation*, 2021 & 2024)
* **Case Citation:** *Cour de cassation, Chambre criminelle*, **7 September 2021, No. 19-87.367** (`ECLI:FR:CCASS:2021:CR00868`), affirmed on **16 January 2024, No. 22-83.681**.
* **NGO Champions:** **Sherpa** (Paris) and the **European Center for Constitutional and Human Rights (ECCHR, Berlin)**, alongside 11 former Syrian employees as *parties civiles*.
* **Landmark Legal Holding:** Lower courts had initially dismissed the charge of "complicity in crimes against humanity" against the French cement multinational Lafarge on the ground that the company acted solely out of commercial profit motives rather than ideological sympathy with ISIS. On **7 September 2021**, the Criminal Chamber of the French Supreme Court (*Cour de cassation*) overturned that dismissal in a watershed ruling of international criminal law, holding that under **Article 121-7 of the French Penal Code**:
  > *"Complicity in crimes against humanity does not require that the accomplice belong to the organization or share the principal perpetrator's design to commit a widespread or systematic attack against a civilian population; **it suffices that the accomplice has knowledge (connaissance) that, through its aid or assistance, it facilitates the preparation or commission of such acts**."*
* **Direct Transposition to Minab:** Neither **Palantir Technologies** nor defense hardware suppliers can excuse complicity by claiming they merely fulfilled commercial software or defense contracts for profit without intending to harm Iranian schoolchildren. Under the binding *Lafarge* standard, **knowledge that deploying an automated AI target-generation system (Maven) at >1,000 targets per 24 hours without civilian-harm guardrails creates a foreseeable risk of unlawful strikes on civilian objects suffices to establish criminal complicity**.

#### Case Benchmark F-3: *Amesys (Bull/Atos) & Nexa Technologies* — Criminal Indictment of Software Executives (*Pôle crimes contre l'humanité*, 2011–Present)
* **NGO Champions:** **International Federation for Human Rights (FIDH)** and **Ligue des droits de l'Homme (LDH)**.
* **Factual & Legal Holding:** Following *partie civile* complaints filed by FIDH and LDH in 2011 and 2017 regarding the supply of deep-packet inspection and mass-surveillance software (*Eagle* to Libya and *Cerebro* to Egypt), Paris investigating judges indicted **four senior software executives** in **June 2021** for **complicity in torture and enforced disappearance**.
* **Direct Transposition to Minab:** *Amesys/Nexa* proves that European war-crimes magistrates do not limit complicity to physical bombs—**software architectures and algorithmic systems** supplied to military/intelligence apparatuses with reckless disregard for civilian safeguards expose both corporate entities and responsible executives to criminal indictment. Furthermore, Palantir's admission (reported by Bloomberg, `EXH-CENTCOM-004.4`) that it deployed **post-strike software patches** to Maven to add automated civilian-disqualification checks proves *ex post* that the company possessed the technical control to build those guardrails *ex ante* and failed to do so.

#### Case Benchmark F-4: *French Corporate Duty of Vigilance Law* (*Loi n° 2017-399 relative au devoir de vigilance*)
* **Legal Basis:** French Commercial Code Arts. L. 225-102-4 and L. 225-102-5 (interpreted by the Paris Court of Appeal, Newly Created Chamber 5-12, 18 June 2024 in *TotalEnergies*, *EDF*, and *Vigie*).
* **Mechanism:** Requires qualifying corporations operating in France to publish and effectively implement a **Vigilance Plan (*Plan de vigilance*)** mapping and preventing severe human-rights violations across their supply chain and downstream product use, enforceable by civil injunction (*mise en demeure*) and tort damages before the Paris Judicial Court.

### B. THE NETHERLANDS: Specialized War Crimes Prosecution (*TIM*) & Extraterritorial Civil Tort / Injunctive Jurisdiction (The Hague)

#### Why The Hague Is PFPJ ry's Strongest Dual-Track Forum
1. **Direct PFPJ ry Board Presence in The Hague:** Two of PFPJ ry's three Founding Board members—**Dr. Zahra Azhar** (Board Member & International Legal Lead; Leiden University researcher and former legal assistant at the Iran–United States Claims Tribunal in The Hague) and **Dr. Ali Akbar Siapoush** (Board Member, PhD in Public International Law)—reside in **The Hague, Netherlands**.
2. **Dual Criminal & Civil Jurisdiction:** Dutch law combines one of the world's most active specialized police/prosecutorial units—the **International Crimes Team (*Team Internationale Misdrijven* — TIM)** of the National Police (*Korps Landelijke Politiediensten*) and the National Office of the Public Prosecution Service (*Landelijk Parket*) under the **International Crimes Act (*Wet internationale misdrijven* — WIM)**—with broad extraterritorial **civil tort jurisdiction** under **Article 6:162 of the Dutch Civil Code (*Burgerlijk Wetboek* — BW)** and **Article 3:305a BW** (which explicitly grants public-interest foundations and associations standing to bring collective actions for declaratory and injunctive relief).

#### Case Benchmark NL-1: *Public Prosecutor v. Frans van Anraat* & *Guus Kouwenhoven* (Dutch Supreme Court, 2009 & 2017)
* **Citations:**
  - *Van Anraat:* The Hague Court of Appeal, 9 May 2007 (`ECLI:NL:GHSGR:2007:BA4676`), affirmed by the Dutch Supreme Court (*Hoge Raad*), 30 June 2009 (`ECLI:NL:HR:2009:BG4822`) — **17 years' imprisonment** for complicity in war crimes (supplying chemical precursors used in aerial bombardments in Halabja and Iran).
  - *Kouwenhoven:* 's-Hertogenbosch Court of Appeal, 21 April 2017 (`ECLI:NL:GHSHE:2017:1760`) — **19 years' imprisonment** for complicity in war crimes by providing weapons and logistical infrastructure in Liberia.
* **Landmark Legal Holding:** Dutch courts apply the ***voorwaardelijk opzet* (*dolus eventualis* / conditional intent)** standard to corporate suppliers: a vendor is criminally complicit in war crimes if it **consciously accepted the substantial chance (*aanmerkelijke kans*)** that its hardware, precursors, or logistical systems would be used to commit attacks violating the laws and customs of war, and supplied them nonetheless.

#### Case Benchmark NL-2: *The Hawija (2015) & Chora (2007) Airstrike Civil Tort Litigation* (District Court of The Hague, 2022–2025)
* **Citations:**
  - *Hawija Airstrike Case:* District Court of The Hague (*Rechtbank Den Haag*), Interlocutory Judgments of **17 January 2024 (`ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2024:329`)** and **December 2024 (`ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2024:22140`)**, Docket `C/09/625957 / HA ZA 22-219`.
  - *Chora Artillery/Airstrike Case:* District Court of The Hague, **23 November 2022 (`ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2022:12628`)** — holding the State liable in civil tort (`Art. 6:162 BW`) and awarding financial compensation to Afghan civilian victims because military commanders failed to verify whether the target remained a military objective at the time of the strike.
* **NGO & Legal Champions:** Litigated by **Prof. Liesbeth Zegveld** (**Prakken d'Oliveira Human Rights Lawyers**, Amsterdam) with support from the **Nuhanovic Foundation (Center for War Reparations)** and **PAX**.
* **Factual Parallel to Minab:** In the night of 2–3 June 2015, Dutch F-16 jets operating within the U.S.-led coalition (CENTCOM / Operation Inherent Resolve) bombed an industrial target in Hawija, Iraq, based on U.S./coalition target folders that underestimated explosive yield and failed to account for surrounding civilian residences, killing ~85 civilians and injuring >500.
* **Procedural Breakthrough:** Rejecting the Ministry of Defense's "state secrets / operational discretion" shield, **The Hague District Court ordered the military to produce internal targeting logs, target-folder inputs, and collateral damage estimates** (`ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2024:329`), directly precipitating the independent **Sorgdrager Commission Inquiry (2025)** and an official **State Apology (March 2025)**.
* **Direct Transposition to Minab:** The *Chora* and *Hawija* rulings establish that Dutch courts treat breaches of **Additional Protocol I Article 57 (feasible precautions in target verification)** as directly actionable torts under **Article 6:162 BW** and compel judicial discovery of targeting intelligence.

#### Case Benchmark NL-3: *Oxfam Novib, PAX & The Rights Forum v. The State of the Netherlands (F-35 Parts Injunction)* (The Hague Court of Appeal, 2024)
* **Citation:** The Hague Court of Appeal (*Gerechtshof Den Haag*), **12 February 2024 (`ECLI:NL:GHDHA:2024:191`)**.
* **Legal Holding:** Brought by three NGOs under **Article 3:305a BW** (collective public-interest action), the Court of Appeal ordered an immediate halt within 7 days to the export of U.S.-owned F-35 fighter jet components stored at the Woensdrecht logistics warehouse in the Netherlands due to a "clear risk" (*duidelijk risico*) of serious violations of International Humanitarian Law, directly enforcing **Common Article 1 of the Geneva Conventions** and the **Arms Trade Treaty**.

#### Case Benchmark NL-4: *Al-Haq / Lima Holding B.V. (Riwal)* Corporate War Crimes Investigation (2010–2013)
* **NGO & Legal Champions:** **Al-Haq** and **Prakken d'Oliveira** (Amsterdam).
* **Outcome:** Criminal complaint against a Dutch-registered holding company (*Lima Holding B.V.*) for supplying mobile cranes used in constructing the West Bank barrier triggered criminal searches by the Dutch **International Crimes Team (TIM)** at corporate offices in Dordrecht, compelling the company to restructure and permanently divest all operations in the conflict zone. *(Notably, Allient/Premotec—the European corporate sister of **Globe Motors [`CAGE 55741`]**—is likewise domiciled in **Dordrecht, Netherlands**, while **Palantir** anchors its EU Legal & Compliance Hub in **Amsterdam** and **Collins Aerospace/RTX** operates in **Maastricht, Houten, and Almere**).*

### C. GERMANY: Federal Public Prosecutor Structural Investigations (*Strukturermittlungsverfahren*) & Supply-Chain Complicity

#### Case Benchmark DE-1: *Strukturermittlungsverfahren* under Section 1 of the *Völkerstrafgesetzbuch (VStGB)*
* **Legal Mechanism:** Under **Section 1 of the German Code of Crimes Against International Law (*VStGB*)** and **Section 152(2) of the Code of Criminal Procedure (*StPO*)**, the **Federal Public Prosecutor General (*Generalbundesanwalt beim Bundesgerichtshof* — GBA)** in Karlsruhe has authority to open a **structural investigation (*Strukturermittlungsverfahren*)** into war crimes committed abroad **even when no individual suspect is currently on German territory** (*§ 153f StPO*).
* **Why This Matters for Cost & Discovery:** In the Syrian (*Al-Khatib / Anwar R.*) and Ukrainian structural investigations, the GBA used German Federal Criminal Police (*Bundeskriminalamt — BKA*, Central Unit for the Fight against War Crimes [*ZBKV*]) budgets to ingest NGO open-source dossiers, take sworn witness depositions across Europe, and issue sealed European and international arrest warrants so that any implicated targeter, commander, or contractor executive entering the Schengen Area can be arrested on arrival.

#### Case Benchmark DE-2: *Bin Ali Jaber v. Federal Republic of Germany (The Ramstein Drone Relay Case)* (OVG Münster, 2019; BVerfG, 2025)
* **Citations:** Higher Administrative Court of North Rhine-Westphalia (*Oberverwaltungsgericht Münster*), **19 March 2019 (`4 A 1361/15`)**; Federal Administrative Court (*BVerwG*), **25 November 2020 (`6 C 7.19`)**; Federal Constitutional Court (*Bundesverfassungsgericht*), **July 2025 (`2 BvR 508/21`)**.
* **NGO Champions:** **European Center for Constitutional and Human Rights (ECCHR, Berlin)** and **Reprieve (London)**.
* **Strategic Lesson:** While the *BVerfG* ultimately narrowed the constitutional duty of protection against foreign sovereign acts unless a specific territorial nexus threshold is met, the 10-year litigation forced judicial scrutiny of U.S. CENTCOM targeting data relays transiting European infrastructure. In Minab, **Palantir Technologies GmbH** (Munich) and **Raytheon Deutschland GmbH** (Freising) are direct German corporate entities subject to German corporate, export, and criminal jurisdiction without sovereign immunity.

### D. SWEDEN (NORDIC ANCHOR): State-Funded Prosecution of Corporate Executives & Multi-Million Forfeiture

#### Case Benchmark SE-1: *Prosecutor v. Ian Lundin and Alex Schneiter (The Lundin Energy / Lundin Oil War Crimes Trial)* (Stockholm District Court & Swedish Supreme Court, 2021–2026)
* **Citations:** Stockholm District Court (*Stockholms tingsrätt*), Case No. **`B 11304-14`** (Indictment filed 11 November 2021; Trial 2023–2026); Swedish Supreme Court (*Högsta domstolen*), Decision of **10 November 2022, Case No. `Ö 1314-22` (*NJA 2022 s. 796*)**.
* **How an NGO Report Triggered a State-Funded Mega-Trial:**
  1. In **June 2010**, the European Coalition on Oil in Sudan (**ECOS**, a small NGO coalition) published a factual investigation report titled ***Unpaid Debt***, documenting how Swedish oil company *Lundin Oil* relied on military forces to clear oil exploration Block 5A in southern Sudan (1997–2003), resulting in indiscriminate attacks on civilians.
  2. Based on that NGO dossier, the **Swedish International Public Prosecution Office (*Internationella åklagarkammaren*)** and the **Swedish Police War Crimes Unit (*Krigsbrottskommissionen*)** opened a public criminal investigation.
  3. **The Swedish State paid 100% of the investigative and trial costs:** Over 11 years, Swedish public prosecutors interviewed more than 260 witnesses across Europe, Africa, and North America, compiled an 80,000-page investigation file (*förundersökningsprotokoll*), indicted Chairman **Ian Lundin** and CEO **Alex Schneiter** for **complicity in grave war crimes** (*medhjälp till grovt folkrättsbrott*, Swedish Penal Code Ch. 22 § 6), and sought **SEK 2.4 billion (~€210 million) in corporate forfeiture** from Lundin Energy, alongside civil damages for 32 injured/bereaved Sudanese plaintiffs (*målsägande*, represented at state legal-aid expense by Swedish Victim Counsel *målsägandebiträde*).
  4. On **10 November 2022 (`Ö 1314-22`)**, the **Swedish Supreme Court (*Högsta domstolen*)** issued a landmark precedent affirming that **universal jurisdiction in Sweden applies even against a foreign corporate executive (Swiss national Alex Schneiter) who does not reside in Sweden**.
* **Direct Lesson for PFPJ ry in Helsinki & the Nordics:**
  * *Lundin Energy* is the ultimate proof of concept for Maria Mekri's question: **an NGO does not need €10 million to litigate a war-crimes case.** The NGO's role is to produce the rigorous, court-grade evidentiary dossier (just as ECOS produced *Unpaid Debt*, and PFPJ ry has produced *Docket PFPJ-2026-IR-001* + *UN Doc. A/HRC/63/61*) and hand it over to a European War Crimes Unit with a corporate or jurisdictional nexus. Once the prosecutor opens the file, the **State assumes the multi-million-euro burden**, and victims receive **state-funded victim counsel (*målsägandebiträde*)**.

### E. UNITED KINGDOM & ITALY: CAGE-Code Criminal Complaints & Export Judicial Review

#### Case Benchmark UK-1: *R (Campaign Against Arms Trade) v. Secretary of State for International Trade* ([2019] EWCA Civ 1020)
* **Citation:** Court of Appeal of England and Wales (Civil Division), **20 June 2019, `[2019] EWCA Civ 1020`** (Sir Terence Etherton MR, Irwin LJ, Singh LJ).
