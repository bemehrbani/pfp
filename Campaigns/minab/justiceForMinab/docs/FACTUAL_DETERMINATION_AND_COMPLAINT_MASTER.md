# SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION & EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### 1.1 Mandate of the Complaining Body
This Factual Determination and Criminal Complaint is submitted by **People for Peace & Justice ry (PFPJ ry)**, an independent, registered non-governmental human rights organization incorporated under the Finnish Associations Act (*Yhdistyslaki 503/1989*) with the Finnish Patent and Registration Office (PRH) under Business Identity Code **3616815-5** in Helsinki, Finland. The institutional mandate of PFPJ ry is to investigate, verify, and judicially advance accountability for gross violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL), specifically attacks against protected civilian and educational infrastructure.

### 1.2 The Incident: Opening Salvo Airstrike on Shajareh Tayyebeh School
On February 28, 2026, during the opening hour of international armed conflict, a precision-guided US Navy **BGM-109 Tomahawk Land Attack Cruise Missile (TLAM)** carrying a 1,000-pound (454 kg) WDU-36/B unitary blast-fragmentation warhead struck the central classroom wing of the **Shajareh Tayyebeh Girls' Elementary School** in Minab, Hormozgan Province, Iran (`27.1432° N, 57.0784° E`). The strike occurred while classes were in active morning session, resulting in **156 certified deaths** (including 120 verified female primary students aged 7–12 and 26 educators and staff) and the severe wounding and permanent maiming of over 95 others.

### 1.3 Rebuttal of CENTCOM's "Targeting Coordinate Error"
Statements issued by the US Department of Defense and CENTCOM characterized the strike as an unintentional error arising from outdated pre-2016 Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) records identifying the coordinate as an auxiliary facility of the adjacent Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Naval Logistics Base. PFPJ ry rejects this characterization as a matter of fact and law. Relying on decade-old peacetime data to launch a heavy-yield weapon against a facility registered in public educational databases and physically partitioned by concrete walls since 2016 constitutes **systemic command negligence and willful blindness (*dolus eventualis*)**, rather than a legally permissible mistake of fact.

Launching this weapon at a target 42 meters from an active classroom made the destruction of the school building a physical certainty.

# SECTION 4: APPLICABLE INTERNATIONAL HUMANITARIAN LAW & EDUCATIONAL SANCTUARY

### 4.1 The Principle of Distinction & Presumption of Civilian Status
* **Additional Protocol I, Article 48:** Basic rule requiring constant distinction between civilian objects and military objectives.
* **Additional Protocol I, Article 52(3):** Clear legal presumption: *"In case of doubt whether an object which is normally dedicated to civilian purposes, such as a place of worship, a house or other dwelling or a school, is being used to make an effective contribution to military action, it shall be presumed not to be so used."*

### 4.2 The Safe Schools Declaration & International Sanctuary
Educational facilities possess heightened protected status under Customary IHL (Rule 10), Geneva Convention IV (Article 53), the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (Article 38), and the Safe Schools Declaration, mandating proactive safeguards to shield children from the effects of kinetic operations.

# SECTION 5: LEGAL LIABILITY, SYSTEMIC NEGLIGENCE & WEAPONS FORENSICS

<a id="section-5-1"></a>
### 5.1 Failure of Continuous Target Verification (The Al-Jina Precedent)
Under **AP I Article 57(2)(a)(i)** and **Customary IHL Rule 15**, targeting authorities bear an affirmative, non-delegable duty to do everything feasible to verify target character. As established in **Case 04 (*Al-Jina Mosque Strike*, UN CoI 2017, A/HRC/36/55)**:
* Target verification is a **continuous temporal process**; reliance on stale 2013 databases represents operational negligence.
* **Proximity does not excuse verification:** Physical adjacency to a military installation does not dilute, but rather magnifies, the duty to verify the distinct civilian character of adjacent structures.

<a id="section-5-2"></a>
### 5.2 Rejection of the "Fog of War" Defense on Day 1 (The Dora Farms Precedent)
Citing **Case 06 (*Dora Farms*, HRW 2003)** and **Case 01 (*Belgrade Embassy*, ICTY OTP 2000)**, targeting errors originating from pre-planned nomination lists compiled in peacetime reflect systemic negligence, legally stripping commanders of the "fog of war" excuse.

<a id="section-5-2b"></a>
### 5.2b Precision-Guided Ballistics & Upstream Culpability (The Belgrade Standard)
In **Case 01 (*Belgrade Chinese Embassy Bombing*, ICTY OTP Final Report §§ 80–85)**, the Prosecutor established that:
1. **Zero Ballistic Drift:** Where precision satellite-guided munitions (JDAM/Tomahawk) are used, the weapon functions with mechanical perfection, hitting its programmed coordinate. This legally refutes any defense of weapon malfunction or pilot inaccuracy.
2. **Locus of Criminal Liability (§ 85):** Autonomous guidance exonerates the launch crew and concentrates 100% of culpability upon the intelligence nominators and target review boards.
3. **DSMAC Optical Sensor Failure:** Unlike blind GPS JDAMs, the BGM-109 Tomahawk utilizes **DSMAC optical scene-matching** sensors in its terminal phase. Failing to abort upon viewing the physical schoolyard constitutes conscious disregard (*dolus eventualis*).

<a id="section-5-3"></a>
### 5.3 Gross Disproportionality & Inherent Breach in Choice of Means/Methods (*Isayeva* & *Dahyan*)

Russia*, ECtHR 2005, §§ 189–191):** The ECtHR established that deploying heavy high-explosive ordnance (FAB-250/500) within or adjacent to a populated civilian settlement is *"impossible to reconcile with the degree of caution expected"* and violates the standard of care, regardless of claimed adversary presence.
* **Child Sanctuary (Case 15: *Dahyan School Bus*, UN GEE 2018):** Launching precision heavy blast weapons where children are gathered cannot be excused as "collateral damage"; it is an indiscriminate attack under **AP I Art. 51(5)(b)** and a war crime under **Rome Statute Art. 8(2)(b)(iv)**.

<a id="section-5-4"></a>
### 5.4 Command Confirmation Bias & Remote Sensor Blindness (*Kabul Drone Strike*)

<a id="section-6-6"></a>
### 6.6 State Responsibility for Internationally Wrongful Acts (ILC ARSIWA Doctrine)
Beyond individual criminal accountability, the Minab school strike directly triggers the international responsibility of the United States as a sovereign State under customary international law, as codified in the **International Law Commission's Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts (ARSIWA, 2001)**:
* **Attribution to the State (Articles 4 & 8 ARSIWA):** The target nomination, vetting cycle, and kinetic launch of BGM-109 Tomahawk cruise missiles were carried out exclusively by official organs of the United States (the US Navy, DIA, and CENTCOM). Under Article 4, conduct of any State organ is considered an act of that State under international law.
* **Breach of Primary IHL Obligations (Article 2 ARSIWA):** Launching high-yield precision blast munitions into an active elementary educational complex violates the customary and conventional international obligations of the United States under Common Article 1 and Article 53 of the Fourth Geneva Convention, as well as Customary IHL Rules 1, 7, 10, 14, and 15.
* **Serious Breaches of Peremptory Norms (*Jus Cogens*) (Articles 40–41 ARSIWA):** The prohibition against targeting child civilian sanctuaries and active educational centers is a peremptory norm of general international law. Under Article 41:
  1. No State (including European and regional partners) shall recognize as lawful a situation created by a serious breach;
  2. No State shall render aid or assistance in maintaining that situation; and
  3. All States hold a positive legal duty to cooperate through lawful means to bring the breach to an end.

### 6.7 Non-Criminal Jurisdictional Fora & Transboundary Human Rights Obligations
PFPJ ry identifies three non-criminal, state-level mechanisms to pursue state accountability and full reparations:
1. **International Court of Justice (ICJ):**
   * *State-to-State Contentious Jurisdiction (ICJ Statute Art. 36):* Iran possesses legal standing to institute contentious proceedings before the ICJ for breaches of customary international law and treaty protections shielding civilian sanctuaries.
   * *UN General Assembly Advisory Opinion:* Civil society coalitions and UN Member States can sponsor a UN General Assembly resolution requesting an Advisory Opinion from the ICJ on the legal consequences of precision standoff kinetic strikes on protected educational infrastructure during the opening hours of international armed conflict.
2. **Extraterritorial Human Rights Jurisdiction (UN Treaty Bodies):**
   * *UN Human Rights Committee (ICCPR):* Pursuant to General Comment No. 36 (2018) on Article 6 (Right to Life, § 63), a State’s jurisdictional obligations encompass individuals outside its sovereign territory whose right to life is affected by its military kinetic activities in a direct and reasonably foreseeable manner.
   * *UN Committee on the Rights of the Child (CRC):* Under the transboundary harm doctrine recognized in *Sacchi et al. v. Argentina* (2021), a State exercises jurisdiction where acts under its direct sovereign control cause foreseeable physical and educational devastation to children extraterritorially.
3. **Administrative & Diplomatic Reparation Mechanisms:**
   * *State Condolence & Claims Precedents:* Independent of criminal guilt, public international law recognizes state liability requiring formal satisfaction, *ex gratia* compensation (e.g., following the precedent of the 1988 USS *Vincennes* / Iran Air 655 ICJ settlement), and the establishment of an international claims commission for child victims.
