§ 1 — Name and Domicile

The name of the association is People for Peace & Justice and, once registered,
it shall bear the suffix ry.

The domicile of the association is Helsinki, Finland.


§ 2 — Purpose

The association's purpose is to advance and promote justice and accountability
for violations of international human rights law and international humanitarian
law, with particular focus on protecting the rights of children and civilians
affected by state violence and armed conflict.


§ 3 — Forms of Activity

The association fulfils its purpose by:

  1. Legal research and documentation of human rights violations, war crimes,
     and crimes against humanity, with emphasis on evidence-gathering and
     fact-finding methodologies that meet international legal standards.

  2. Advocacy and public awareness campaigns to inform the international
     community, governments, and judicial bodies about documented violations
     and the need for accountability.

  3. Support for victims and affected communities by facilitating access to
     legal remedies, connecting victims with legal representation, and
     coordinating with international judicial mechanisms.

  4. Engagement with international justice institutions including but not
     limited to the International Criminal Court (ICC), the International
     Court of Justice (ICJ), and UN treaty bodies.

  5. Knowledge sharing and capacity building through publications, educational
     programs, and partnerships with peer organizations in the international
     justice ecosystem.

  6. Local engagement and community empowerment by connecting affected
     communities with documentation, legal, and advocacy resources.

  7. All other lawful means directly or indirectly conducive to the
     realization of the association's objectives.

To support its activities, the association may receive donations, grants, and
subsidies; organize fundraising events and campaigns; publish educational and
research materials; and engage in minor merchandise sales. The association may
own property necessary for its activities.


§ 4 — Membership

Any natural person or legal entity that accepts the association's purpose and
rules may become a member upon approval by the board of directors.

The board of directors may grant honorary membership to an individual who has
significantly contributed to the association's mission. Honorary members are
exempt from membership fees.

A member may resign from the association by submitting a written notice to the
board of directors or by declaring their resignation at a meeting of the
association, to be recorded in the minutes.

The board of directors may expel a member who has failed to pay a due
membership fee, has acted contrary to the association's purpose or rules, or
has significantly harmed the association's interests. Before expulsion, the
member shall be given the opportunity to be heard.


§ 5 — Membership Fee

The annual membership fee shall be decided by the annual meeting of the
association.

Honorary members are not required to pay membership fees.


§ 6 — Board of Directors

The association's affairs are managed by a board of directors consisting of
a chairperson and 2 to 6 other members.

The board of directors is elected at the annual meeting for a term of one (1)
calendar year.

The board of directors elects from among its members a vice-chairperson and
appoints a secretary and a treasurer, who may also be persons outside the
board.

The board of directors convenes when summoned by the chairperson or, in their
absence, the vice-chairperson, or when at least half of the board members
request a meeting. The board has a quorum when at least half of its members,
including the chairperson or vice-chairperson, are present.

Board meetings may be held in person, by teleconference, video conference, or
other electronic means, or as a combination thereof (hybrid meeting).


§ 7 — Signing Rights

The association's name is signed by the chairperson alone, or by any two (2)
board members together.


§ 8 — Accounting Period and Audit

The accounting period of the association is the calendar year
(January 1 – December 31).

The association shall have one (1) operations inspector and one (1) deputy
operations inspector, elected at the annual meeting for a term of one
calendar year.

The annual accounts shall be submitted to the operations inspector at least
one (1) month before the annual meeting. The operations inspector shall
submit their report to the board at least two (2) weeks before the annual
meeting.


§ 9 — Meetings of the Association

The association holds one (1) annual meeting per calendar year, to be held
by the end of March.

An extraordinary meeting shall be held when the board considers it necessary,
or when at least one-tenth (1/10) of the members entitled to vote submit a
written request to the board for a specific matter to be addressed.

Meetings may be held in person, by teleconference, video conference, or other
electronic means, or as a combination thereof (hybrid meeting). A fully remote
meeting may be held if decided by the board when convening the meeting.


§ 10 — Convening Meetings

The meeting notice shall be sent to members at least fourteen (14) days before
the meeting by email to the address provided by each member, or by publishing
the notice on the association's website.


§ 11 — Annual Meeting Agenda

At the annual meeting, the following matters shall be addressed:

   1.  Opening of the meeting
   2.  Election of the meeting chairperson, secretary, two minutes inspectors,
       and, if necessary, two vote counters
   3.  Confirmation that the meeting has been lawfully convened and has a
       quorum
   4.  Approval of the agenda
   5.  Presentation of the annual accounts, the annual report, and the
       operations inspector's report
   6.  Decision on the adoption of the annual accounts and the granting of
       discharge from liability to the board and other persons accountable
   7.  Approval of the action plan, budget, and membership fees for the
       current year
   8.  Election of the chairperson and other members of the board
   9.  Election of the operations inspector and deputy operations inspector
  10.  Other matters raised in the meeting notice

If a member wishes to have a matter addressed at the annual meeting, they
shall submit a written request to the board at least thirty (30) days before
the meeting.


§ 12 — Voting

In meetings of the association, each member has one (1) vote.

Decisions are made by a simple majority of votes cast, unless otherwise
required by these rules or the Associations Act.

In the event of a tie, the chairperson of the meeting has the casting vote,
except in elections, which shall be decided by lot.


§ 13 — Amendment of Rules

A decision to amend these rules requires a majority of three-quarters (3/4)
of the votes cast at a meeting of the association.


§ 14 — Dissolution

A decision to dissolve the association requires a majority of three-quarters
(3/4) of the votes cast at two consecutive meetings, held at least thirty (30)
days apart.

Upon dissolution, the association's remaining assets shall be used for a
purpose that advances the association's objectives, as determined by the last
meeting deciding on the dissolution. Assets shall be transferred to a
registered association, foundation, or public body engaged in human rights or
international justice work.


§ 15 — Other Matters

In all matters not addressed by these rules, the provisions of the Finnish
Associations Act (Yhdistyslaki) shall apply.


These rules were adopted at the founding meeting of the association
on _______, 2026.
