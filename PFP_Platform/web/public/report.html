SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION & EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.1 This Factual Determination and Complaint is submitted by People for Peace & Justice ry (PFPJ ry), a registered non-governmental organization incorporated in Helsinki, Finland (Business ID: 3616815-5). The mandate of this filing is to compile, verify, and submit evidence establishing command negligence and violation of the duty of precaution under International Humanitarian Law (IHL) in relation to the devastating airstrike conducted on February 28, 2026, against the Shajareh Tayyebeh Girls' Elementary School in Minab, Iran.

1.2 On February 28, 2026, during the opening hours of military operations, a precision-guided US BGM-109 Tomahawk cruise missile struck the central wing of the Shajareh Tayyebeh school complex. At the time of the impact, the school was in active session with approximately 300 children present. The strike resulted in 156 certified deaths (including 120 young schoolchildren and 26 educators/staff) and the severe wounding of over 95 others, representing one of the single largest child civilian casualty events of modern aerial warfare.

1.3 Preliminary statements from the US Department of Defense and the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) characterized the strike as a "tragic coordinates error" resulting from mistaken targeting of the adjacent Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) naval logistics base. PFPJ ry rejects this defense. Investigation of the targeting cycle proves that the strike planner office utilized outdated or unverified coordinate databases, failing to verify target usage changes. Under international law, this constitutes systemic planning negligence, rather than a permissible "fog of war" mistake.

SECTION 2: INVESTIGATIVE METHODOLOGY & EVIDENCE VERIFICATION

2.1 The findings in this dossier are established using high-standard Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) methodology, satellite imagery analysis, flight tracking telemetry, weapons fragmentation verification, and cross-verification of victim registries. Digital chain-of-custody was maintained for all geolocation logs and victim profiles to ensure compliance with European prosecutorial standards.

2.2 PFPJ ry has compiled a memorial database containing the profiles of the victims, including the geolocated remains, photos, and birth records of 100 verified children killed in the blast. Visual assets were authenticated using shadow chronolocation and metadata analysis.

"Verification of child casualties in school airstrikes must adhere to strict spatial and chronolocational standards to eliminate doubts regarding military presence."
Cross-Ref Precedent: Case 13 (Hass School Strike, Syria, 2016)

SECTION 3: CHRONOLOGY & GEOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS

3.1 The Shajareh Tayyebeh Girls' School is located at GPS coordinates 27.1432 N, 57.0784 E in Minab, Hormozgan Province. The school property shares a northern boundary fence with the Minab IRGC Naval Base. High-resolution satellite chronologies from 2015 to 2026 prove that the school facility has been continuously active for over a decade, featuring distinct playground structures, a sports courtyard, and visible daily assemblies of children wearing bright uniforms.

3.2 Peacetime records show that the school was officially registered with the Ministry of Education and listed in public municipal databases. The geographic separation between the school's central building and the nearest military warehouse inside the base is exactly 42 meters, which is well within the known lethal blast radius of a heavy Tomahawk cruise missile carrying a 1,000 lb warhead.

3.3 The chronological log of the strike on February 28, 2026, shows that the missile was launched during the opening hour of hostilities. This indicates the target was drawn from a pre-planned Target Nomination List compiled weeks or months prior to the outbreak of war, during peacetime conditions when operators had ample time and resources to conduct vetting.

SECTION 4: APPLICABLE INTERNATIONAL LAW

4.1 Under Customary International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and Additional Protocol I (Article 48), the Principle of Distinction requires conflicting parties to distinguish between civilian objects and military objectives. Section 52(3) establishes a clear legal presumption: in case of doubt whether an object normally dedicated to civilian purposes, such as a school, is being used to make an effective contribution to military action, it must be presumed not to be so used.

4.2 Furthermore, the Safe Schools Declaration, to which international coalitions adhere, mandates heightened precautions when conducting operations in proximity to educational infrastructure to prevent disruption of children's rights to education and safety.

SECTION 5: LEGAL LIABILITY & NEGLECT OF PRECAUTION

5.1 Failure of Continuous Target Verification (Duty of Precaution): Under AP I Article 57(2)(a)(i) and Customary IHL Rule 15, targeting authorities bear an affirmative, continuous duty to verify that objectives are neither civilians nor civilian objects. Target nominators relied on stale 2013 intelligence, failing to verify coordinate usage shifts over a decade despite public registration and physical wall partitioning.

"Failing to track target coordinate usage changes over time and striking newly constructed or active civilian structures constitutes a breach of the duty of precaution. Proximity to a military objective compounds rather than excuses the duty to verify."
Cross-Ref Precedent: Case 04 (Al-Jina Mosque Strike, UN CoI 2017)

5.2 Rejection of the "Fog of War" / "Honest Mistake" Defense: Because this strike occurred on Day 1 of the international armed conflict, the target list was pre-planned during peacetime. The defense of rapid, high-pressure tactical adjustments in combat does not apply to target nominators working in peacetime intelligence offices weeks prior to launch.

"Targeting errors originating from pre-planned nomination lists compiled during peacetime reflect systematic negligence, stripping commanders of the 'fog of war' excuse."
Cross-Ref Precedents: Case 01 (Belgrade Embassy, 1999) & Case 06 (Dora Farms, 2003)

5.2b Precision-Guided Ballistics & Upstream Culpability (The Belgrade Standard): As established by the ICTY OTP in the Belgrade Embassy investigation (Case 01, §§ 80–85), autonomous precision guidance eliminates aerodynamic drift or operator error as a legal defense. Debris recovery (EVD-MUN-002) confirms sub-5m terminal accuracy, shifting 100% of liability onto intelligence nominators. Unlike 1999 GPS JDAMs, the Tomahawk's DSMAC optical scene-matching camera should have visually alerted planners to the schoolyard, proving reckless disregard (dolus eventualis).

"When precision-guided weapons function with mechanical perfection, targeting liability shifts entirely away from executing aircrews and onto the intelligence officers who compiled the target coordinate folder."
Cross-Ref Precedent: Case 01 (Belgrade Chinese Embassy, ICTY OTP 2000)

5.3 Inherent Disproportionality & Means/Methods of Warfare: Under AP I Article 57(2)(a)(ii) and Article 51(5)(b), launching a 1,000-lb high-explosive unitary warhead immediately adjacent to an active school during morning class hours violates the duty in choice of means and methods. The catastrophic civilian toll was mathematically certain and grossly excessive in relation to any anticipated concrete military advantage.

"Deploying heavy blast weapons in or adjacent to civilian settlements is impossible to reconcile with the standard of care required in military operations. High precision in munition delivery confirms that targeting child concentrations is the product of gross command recklessness."
Cross-Ref Precedents: Case 03 (Isayeva v. Russia, ECtHR 2005) & Case 15 (Dahyan School Bus, UN GEE 2018)

5.4 Command Confirmation Bias & Remote Sensor Blindness: Relying on high-altitude surveillance without positive ground verification created a fatal cognitive echo chamber. As in the 2021 Kabul drone strike, analysts anchored on a military threat hypothesis, systematically filtering out routine civilian life indicators—including school buses, morning assembly lines, and children in uniform—as operational noise.

"Remote sensor loops under operational stress suffer from cognitive tunnel vision, converting neutral civilian objects into threat signatures and blinding command cells to the presence of children."
Cross-Ref Precedents: Case 05 (Kabul Drone Strike, CENTCOM IG 2021) & Case 13 (Hass School, 2016)

SECTION 6: JURISDICTIONAL LINK & PROCEDURAL OBLIGATION

6.1 Legal Domicile & Territorial Nexus: PFPJ ry is incorporated in Helsinki, Finland (Business ID: 3616815-5), creating an immutable institutional nexus within the legal order of a Council of Europe Member State to document IHL violations and seek judicial redress for victims.

6.2 Universal Jurisdiction under Finnish Penal Code (Rikoslaki): Chapter 1, Section 7 of Rikoslaki (39/1889) extends Finnish criminal jurisdiction unconditionally over international offenses. Chapter 11, Section 6 criminalizes Aggravated War Crimes (Törkeä sotarikos), mandating domestic investigation where mass child casualties result from disproportionate attacks.

6.3 Binding Procedural Obligation under ECHR Article 2 (The Hanan Precedent): Pursuant to the ECtHR Grand Chamber ruling in Hanan v. Germany (2021, §§ 132–145), European states hold an autonomous, detachable procedural obligation to criminally investigate extraterritorial strikes when territorial remedies are blocked and an impunity vacuum exists.

"European states bear a binding procedural obligation under Article 2 ECHR to investigate civilian casualties from strikes when domestic immunity regimes create an impunity gap."
Cross-Ref Precedent: Case 02 (Hanan v. Germany [GC], 2021)

6.4 Comparative European Authority (German GBA & VStGB § 1): Under Section 1 of the German VStGB, universal jurisdiction requires only factual indications of a war crime (Anfangsverdacht under § 152(2) StPO). The certified deaths of 156 civilians legally surpasses this threshold, compelling European prosecutorial action.

6.5 Distinguishing the Kunduz 'Honest Mistake' Defense: In Hanan, the commander operated under fluid, dynamic nighttime combat with 7 real-time informant checks. In Minab, planners executed a Day 1 static target from peacetime nomination lists with zero verification against public educational registries, establishing conscious disregard (dolus eventualis).

SECTION 7: PROSECUTORIAL RECOMMENDATIONS & REMEDIES

7.1 Petition to the Finnish Office of the Prosecutor General: PFPJ ry formally petitions the Finnish Prosecutor General (Valtakunnansyyttäjän toimisto) to issue an investigation order (syyttämismääräys) under Ch. 1 § 12 Rikoslaki for the National Bureau of Investigation (KRP) to initiate pre-trial inquiries into target nomination officers.

7.2 Article 15 Rome Statute Communication to the ICC: PFPJ ry submits this dossier to the ICC Office of the Prosecutor in The Hague under Article 15, alleging war crimes under Article 8(2)(b)(i) (civilian objects), 8(2)(b)(ii) (educational buildings), and 8(2)(b)(iv) (disproportionate attacks).

7.3 Parallel Filings with German GBA & Genocide Network: PFPJ ry shall lodge a parallel criminal complaint (Strafanzeige) with the German Federal Prosecutor General in Karlsruhe under § 1 and § 11(1) no. 3 VStGB, requesting evidence sharing via the European Genocide Network.

7.4 Evidentiary Preservation & Judicial Chain of Custody: Physical missile remnants, serial number fragments, and local RF telecommunications logs are secured under Berkeley Protocol standards in European repositories for immediate transfer upon issuance of letters rogatory (oikeusapupyyntö).

7.5 Enforcement of Victims' Procedural Rights (ECHR Article 13): Demanding unhindered legal access for victims' kin to non-classified dossiers and ensuring any prosecutorial closure is subject to judicial review, vindicating the victims' right to truth and effective remedy.

SECTION 8: MODES OF LIABILITY & UPSTREAM ATTRIBUTION (ICTY § 85)

8.1 Upstream Concentration of Liability: Under Paragraph 85 of the ICTY OTP NATO Report, naval launch personnel on the USS Abraham Lincoln bear zero culpability; 100% of institutional and legal culpability concentrates upstream upon target nominators, DIA database managers, and the CENTCOM Target Review Board.

8.2 Command Responsibility: Senior leadership dismantled civilian harm oversight mechanisms, prioritizing algorithmic speed without human vetting.

8.3 State Responsibility (ILC ARSIWA Arts. 2, 4, 40, 41): The acts of US Navy, CENTCOM, and DIA organs are fully attributable to the United States (Art. 4). The breach of peremptory norms (jus cogens) protecting children triggers an international legal obligation for all States not to recognize the unlawful situation.

SECTION 9: JURISDICTIONAL MECHANISMS & INTER-STATE FORA

9.1 State Responsibility Fora: Recourse through the International Court of Justice (ICJ Contentious Jurisdiction) and UN General Assembly Advisory Opinion procedures.

9.2 Extraterritorial Human Rights Jurisdiction: UN HRC General Comment 36 (Right to Life) and UN Committee on the Rights of the Child transboundary doctrine (Sacchi et al. v. Argentina, 2021).
