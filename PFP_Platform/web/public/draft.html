9. 10. Statutory Rule 89(C) of the ICTY and ICTR Rules of Procedure and Evidence provides:

11. International tribunals do not apply municipal hearsay exclusionary rules. Evidence is admitted provided it possesses two indispensable qualities: (i) relevance to the issues under review, and (ii) probative value, evaluated through reliability, authenticity, and source corroboration (Prosecutor v. § 3.1 to 3.3 The Tadić Principle & International Armed Conflict (Common Art. 2 GC)

14. In Prosecutor v. Duško Tadić (Case No. IT-94-1-AR72, Appeals Chamber, October 2, 1995, § 70), the ICTY Appeals Chamber defined the threshold: "[A]n armed conflict exists whenever there is a resort to armed force between States[...]."

15. Under Common Article 2 of the 1949 Geneva Conventions, to which both the United States and Iran are High Contracting Parties, the deployment of kinetic cruise missiles against sovereign Iranian territory on February 28, 2026, constituted a resort to armed force between States, qualifying ipso jure as an International Armed Conflict (IAC).

16. The operational nexus is established: the three Tomahawk missiles were launched as part of the opening wave of "Operation Epic Fury" directed against sovereign territory.

§ 3.4 & 3.5 The Absolute Presumption of Civilian Status in Doubt (AP I Art. 52(3))

17. Under Additional Protocol I Article 52(1) and Customary IHL Rule 8, civilian objects comprise all objects which are not military objectives. Educational facilities are protected civilian objects par excellence.

"In case of doubt whether an object which is normally dedicated to civilian purposes, such as a place of worship, a house or other dwelling or a school, is being used to make an effective contribution to military action, it shall be presumed not to be so used." (AP I Art. 52(3))

18. Target planners were bound by an affirmative legal obligation to presume that the Shajareh Tayyebeh School was a protected civilian sanctuary. No verified intelligence existed showing any military use.
