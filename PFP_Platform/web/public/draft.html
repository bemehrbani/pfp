§ 3.1 & 3.2 The Tadić Threshold & Factual Establishment of Armed Conflict

18–19. In Prosecutor v. Duško Tadić (Case No. IT-94-1-AR72, Appeals Chamber, October 2, 1995, ¶ 70), the ICTY Appeals Chamber formulated the universally authoritative threshold definition of armed conflict: "[A]n armed conflict exists whenever there is a resort to armed force between States[...]. International humanitarian law applies from the initiation of such armed conflicts and extends beyond the cessation of hostilities until a general conclusion of peace is reached." This standard is incorporated into ICC jurisprudence (Lubanga Trial Judgment, ICC-01/04-01/06-2842, ¶ 533; Katanga Trial Judgment, ICC-01/04-01/07-3436, ¶ 1173; Bemba Decision on the Charges, ICC-01/05-01/08-424, ¶ 229).

20–22. Factual Establishment: Applying the Tadić threshold: (a) the parties are the United States of America and the Islamic Republic of Iran, both sovereign States and High Contracting Parties to the 1949 Geneva Conventions; (b) on February 28, 2026, US armed forces launched kinetic cruise missile strikes from the Gulf of Oman against targets on sovereign Iranian territory; and (c) the strike was directed exclusively by official organs of one State against another State with no intermediary non-state actor. Unlike non-international armed conflict (NIAC), the threshold for an International Armed Conflict (IAC) is triggered by the very first use of inter-state armed force, without requiring any minimum duration or intensity (Bemba, ¶ 223).
